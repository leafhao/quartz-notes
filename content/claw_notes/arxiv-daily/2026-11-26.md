### 📄 [CoT-Proxy: Cross-Format Transfer for Distilling Long-Horizon Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33148)

- **作者**：Dawid J. Kopiczko et al.
- **关键词**：HF 未返回
- **摘要**：Reasoning models become long-horizon agents through reinforcement learning or, more cheaply, through fine-tuning on a stronger teacher's trajectories, which contain the teacher's chain-of-thought as well as its actions. The standard fine-tuning approach imitates both, yet the strongest teachers are closed models whose chain-of-thought is hidden behind an API. We show that the chain-of-thought can be left out altogether and that the long-horizon policy can be distilled from the teacher's actions alone. The student is trained on these trajectories with its reasoning block left empty, while a KL loss on a replay buffer of its own generations limits drift from its initial model. Surprisingly, the policy learned from these trajectories with an empty reasoning block carries over to inference with thinking enabled, where the student reasons in its own style while acting like the teacher, an effect we call cross-format transfer. Distilling the actions of Qwen3.6-27B raises the task success of Qwen3-4B on AppWorld from 7% to 18% and that of Qwen3-32B from 12% to 25%. We further introduce CoT-proxies, short summaries of the teacher's reasoning placed after the empty reasoning block, which raise Qwen3-4B further, to 35%. Since neither actions nor CoT-proxies need the hidden chain-of-thought, the recipe applies unchanged to closed teachers. Distilling GPT-5.6 Luna from its visible outputs and the reasoning summaries its API returns raises Qwen3-4B from 7% to 48% on AppWorld and from 3% to 28% on SWE-bench Verified.

### 📄 [VideoJSON: A Minimal Harness Beats Purpose-Built Memory for Ultra-Long Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28167)
- **标签**：VideoJSON
- **作者**：Wenqi Marshall Guo et al.
- **关键词**：HF 未返回
- **摘要**：Answering questions over days or weeks of video requires searching for scattered clues across long timelines. Existing video agents rely on complex memory graphs and fixed retrieval pipelines, which restrict how the agent can search. We propose VideoJSON, a minimal harness for ultra-long video understanding that keeps the memory simple and lets a coding agent write its own search programs. During indexing, a vision-language model turns each minute of video into a single JSON record containing a dense caption and structured fields such as events, entities, places, and speech transcripts. At query time, the agent writes Python or Bash programs in a sandboxed container to filter, link, and read these records over multiple steps, without task-specific training or raw video access. On four ultra-long video benchmarks, VideoJSON with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash outperforms MAGIC-Video by 7.0, 20.0, and 24.75 points on EgoLifeQA, Ego-R1-Bench, and MM-Lifelong Week, and beats the best prior EgoMemReason score by 12.6 points. Across four policy models of increasing capability, VideoJSON's average score rises by up to 11.58 points over the weakest model, compared with at most 5.50 points for MAGIC-Video. Ablations show that both agentic code search and the structured JSON records drive these gains, while adding hourly summaries, extra tools, or video access changes the average score by less than 1.5 points. In addition, fine-tuning Qwen-3.5-Flash on 64 VideoJSON trajectories from an unrelated 100-hour livestream improves its MM-Lifelong scores by up to 8.25 points. Code will be open-sourced after publication.
- **票数**：8 👍
