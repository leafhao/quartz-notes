### 📄 [TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22115)
- **标签**：星火四代
- **关键词**：MoE, Ascend, speculative decoding, post-training
- **摘要**：We present Xing 4, a series of large language models that extends the TeleChat lineage through coordinated advances in model architecture and training infrastructure on Ascend. This report describes the design principles and systems engineering behind the series, spanning architecture selection, large-scale pretraining, and reasoning and agentic post-training. We study how sparse activation, attention design, model depth and width, and speculative decoding balance model capacity with computation, memory access, and execution overhead. Our pretraining infrastructure builds on MindSpore HyperParallel, combining topology-aware hybrid sharding, efficient distributed optimizer execution, and coordinated management of long-sequence workloads and activation memory. CPU affinity and core isolation further reduce host scheduling interference. Together, these mechanisms support the pretraining of a 285B-parameter model on 8,192 Ascend 910C cards, providing a concrete setting for the architectural and systems studies. For post-training, we introduce TeleGym, a unified agent execution platform that connects execution, evaluation, and trajectory construction to policy optimization. Disaggregated asynchronous execution, bounded oversampling, and partial rollout accommodate heterogeneous task durations while preserving trajectory continuity across policy updates. Together, these advances establish an Ascend-oriented training foundation for the continued development of Xing 4 across model scales and increasingly demanding reasoning and agent workloads.

### 📄 [ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21607)
- **标签**：尺度上限
- **关键词**：VLA, scaling, action DiT, sample efficiency
- **摘要**：Frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in robot control, suggesting that broad pretraining provides knowledge valuable for physical action. Yet most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies still rely on much smaller VLM backbones, leaving this potential largely untapped. We ask whether frontier-scale representations can provide a stronger foundation for robot learning. We introduce ScaleVLA, which connects a 552B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash backbone to a trainable action DiT, and systematically compare VLMs from 2B to 552B under a shared training setup. We evaluate three fundamental capabilities of robot foundation models. In sample efficiency, just 10 demonstrations outperform a 2B backbone trained with 50 (87.8% vs. 85.6%). In generalization, LIBERO-PRO success reaches 45.1%, compared with at most 31.3% for smaller backbones. In precise manipulation, real-world charger insertion improves from 4/10 to 9/10 successes, with gains concentrated in contact-sensitive alignment. Despite its scale, ScaleVLA achieves 216 ms median latency per action chunk. Our findings suggest that frontier VLMs can serve not only as reasoning agents, but also as powerful representational foundations for efficient and generalizable robot policies.

### 📄 [Accelerate Bench: Evaluating Agents for Application-Level Program Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20882)
- **标签**：程序优化基准
- **关键词**：coding agent, program optimization, benchmark
- **摘要**：Application optimization requires coding agents to discover useful changes and coordinate them across a complete program while preserving the required computation. We introduce Accelerate Bench, a benchmark of 32 CPU optimization tasks adapted from scientific applications and supercomputing competitions. Each task provides a working program and specifies the workload, correctness requirements, timing boundaries, and permitted edits, leaving agents to choose where and how to optimize. Public testing supports iterative development, and a separate verifier scores the final submission. On benchmark version 1.1, we evaluate 16 coding agents, each pairing a model with a harness. Their Score@1 ranges from 25.5% to 67.4%. Most of their programs are correct, and the size of the speedup separates them: the two leading agents are at least 2x faster on 22 or more of the 32 tasks, while the other fourteen reach 2x on 8 to 17 and leave 125 of their 412 correct outcomes below 1.25x. Controlled replays show why coordination across an application matters: identical source code runs between 0.29x and 2.12x depending only on build and runtime settings, and one code change is worth 1.01x or 6.4x depending on another. Accelerate Bench pairs executable workloads with agents' programs and trajectories to evaluate both their gains and the process behind them.
- **票数**：18 👍

- **标签**：输出格式
- **关键词**：output format, semantic parsing, silent errors
- **摘要**：A small fine-tuned parser that emits a well-formed but wrong tree fails silently: nothing downstream can tell. We ask whether the output format, a free design choice, controls how often this happens. On the PIZZA ordering benchmark we fine-tune two sub-billion models (Qwen3.5-0.8B and LFM2.5-350M) with LoRA on six lossless encodings of the same target tree, from 44.3 down to 10.9 tokens per order, including two matched-length pairs that vary surface form at fixed length. Across 300 runs (5 training pool sizes, 5 seeds) with a hierarchical bootstrap and Holm correction fixed before the results were read, shortening alone never helps, but writing the target in ordinary words does. The word encoding is the most accurate format on both models (87.5% vs. 85.8% exact match for the native s-expression on Qwen) while emitting 4.1x fewer tokens, and it cuts the silent error rate by 2.3x on Qwen and 1.8x on LFM2, separating at every pool size. Error analysis locates much of the gain in negation. All runs also learn to refuse off-menu input (held out: 95.9-99.0% handled) and to apply in-utterance self-corrections. Strict refusal has a measured price: 2.1-10.1% of real, mostly polite, orders are refused.
